# Scholarly literature sources for the literature map

Research checked 2026-11-01. This note compares documented first-party interfaces for a private, local-first mathematics/physics literature map. It is a source-selection brief, not a substitute for reviewing a provider's then-current terms before shipping an integration.

## Recommendation

Use **OpenAlex as the default citation-neighborhood index**, with **Crossref as DOI/metadata validation and a second citation source**, and **arXiv as a source of physics/mathematics discovery records and licensed reading copies**. Add **OpenCitations** as a small, independent audit/source for open DOI citation edges. Treat **Unpaywall as an optional DOI-to-open-copy locator**, not a search or graph provider. Keep **Semantic Scholar optional pending an explicit license review**: its graph is unusually useful for citation contexts and an `isInfluential` field, but the public agreement limits use to internal non-commercial research/education and contains broad automated-use/data-modification restrictions. Google Search grounding or the Search Researcher Result API can help a separately and explicitly initiated web-search task; neither is Google Scholar nor a dependable scholarly citation graph.

The graph should store two different things:

1. A provider-backed **bibliographic citation edge** (`work A cites work B`) with provider, provider identifiers, retrieved timestamp, raw response/field provenance, and confidence/coverage caveats. It establishes only that a bibliographic record links the works.
2. A separately evidenced **intellectual relationship** (uses, extends, applies, generalises, corrects, influences), supported by direct text from the paper or another identified primary source: quotation or close excerpt, document/version, section/page/paragraph locator, URL/DOI, and the analyst's interpretation. Neither a citation count nor Semantic Scholar's `isInfluential` classifier establishes this claim.

This follows the product requirement that graph edges expose whether they are bibliographic facts, author-stated relationships, or inference. Citation-count disagreement is expected because indexes differ in coverage and matching.

## Google Scholar and Google-managed search

- **No public official Google Scholar API/MCP was found in Google's official documentation checked for this note.** Google Scholar's own help documents Scholar's interface, citation export/alerts, and its crawler/indexing behavior; it describes how Scholar parses bibliographic records and references, but does not publish a sanctioned endpoint, credentials, quotas, or developer terms for extracting its index/profile/citation graph. The absence claim is bounded to the official documentation examined, rather than a proof that no private arrangement exists. Scholar supports human-facing citation export; that does not make an API.
- Google Search's official spam policy defines automated queries to Google Search without express permission as prohibited machine-generated traffic. Do not build a Scholar scraper or use a third-party scraper/MCP as though it were a sanctioned Google endpoint. Google Scholar's indexing instructions are addressed to publishers exposing their own papers to Google's crawler, not granting users a Scholar data API.
- There **is** a Google-managed **Search Researcher Result API** (SRR API): an authenticated Google Search results interface for approved researchers. Eligibility includes affiliation with a degree-granting higher-education institution, a publishable research goal, and no Alphabet/Google affiliation; approved projects receive 1,000 queries/day. Its responses are nearly the same as standard browser Search responses. The official page describes Google Search, not Scholar's curated scholarly index, citation counts, profiles, or forward/backward citation graph. It is therefore not a Scholar substitute for this personal app.
- **Grounding with Google Search** in Gemini/Google Cloud is also not Scholar. It grounds an answer in publicly available general web data and returns grounding support / Search Suggestions. Google requires displaying the support and Search Suggestions under its product terms. It is a web-discovery/synthesis facility, not an enumerable bibliographic corpus or a citation-topology API; it may incur service/model costs, so it must remain behind explicit user initiation and the app's cost controls.

## Bounded local discovery and evidence pipeline

1. **Explicit research start only.** Keep graph browsing/expansion read-only and local. A user starts research on a focal work; show which metadata providers will be called. No background graph navigation calls, paid search grounding, or agent synthesis.
2. **Resolve and preserve the seed.** Resolve DOI first (Crossref/DataCite), then arXiv ID, then exact-title/year/author candidates in OpenAlex. Show candidate matches for ambiguous work identity. Store all aliases and version relations rather than forcing a hidden merge.
3. **Fetch a capped first-hop union.** From the resolved focal work, get its reference neighborhood and forward citations from OpenAlex, with Crossref and (if license is approved) Semantic Scholar / OpenCitations as complementary evidence. Set an initial default cap such as at most 50 outgoing references and 100 incoming candidate records, per source; page only after user requests expansion. Cap total results and API calls in settings. Store source/edge-specific source IDs, raw metadata fields, retrieval time, and completeness status.
4. **Select downstream discovery candidates without Scholar scraping.** Form the union of incoming citers from each opted-in index. Rank for *review* using (a) count of independent indexes that list the edge, (b) source citation counts/percentile within year or field where available, (c) recency, and (d) optional S2 `isInfluential`/context signals only where its license allows. Also include topical/author/later-work searches in OpenAlex or arXiv and references from trusted survey/review papers. This is a recall-oriented shortlist, not proof that the highest-count works are most intellectually influential. Let the user reveal a bounded second hop for a selected candidate (e.g. its later citing works), repeating caps. Citation breadth can reveal major developments without attempting to clone Scholar's corpus.
5. **Find readable primary text.** Resolve official publisher/repository/arXiv landing pages and OA locations (Unpaywall/OpenAlex metadata). Display source/version/license and open the source. For licensed local PDFs, user-provided originals remain the durable input. Do not assume a provider's `openAccessPdf` or full-text link grants text redistribution.
6. **Evidence only after reading.** An analysis record can classify a relationship as author-stated, directly text-supported, or analyst inference. Save a short exact excerpt or close quotation plus exact page/section/paragraph locator, document version/DOI/URL, and rationale. Model-generated summaries and metadata labels can propose leads, but the human-readable claim needs a direct text locator. Keep failed/ambiguous evidence out of the published graph or label it explicitly pending.
7. **Cache privately and keep provenance.** Persist normalized bibliographic records, raw provider response/selected raw fields where terms permit, provider IDs, per-edge provider, fetched timestamp, request/source URL, matching method/confidence, license attribution, and refresh/check status in local SQLite. Cache only provider-permitted fields; keep abstracts/text subject to copyright/license; don't commit databases, PDF copies or generated temp files to Git. Refresh stale records on explicit research refresh, use provider-specified identifiers/contact headers, low request rates/backoff, and report coverage gaps instead of interpreting missing edges as absence of citation.

### Minimum fields for an evidence-backed map

- Work: local work ID, title, authors/author IDs, year/date, venue, DOI/arXiv/other IDs, version relationship, source landing URL, abstract and its provider/license, OA location/license, fetched-at.
- Citation fact: citing-work ID, cited-work ID, provider and provider edge ID when present, provider record URLs, fetched-at, match basis (DOI vs metadata), coverage/source notes.
- Intellectual relation: source/target work IDs, relation label, evidence kind (author-stated/direct-text/inference), quote/excerpt, exact locator, source version and URL/DOI, interpretation, reviewer/status, and any counter-evidence.

## Scope and limitations

This is not a quantitative bibliometrics recommendation and does not imply that an OpenAlex/S2/Crossref count is “the” citation count. Provider indexes and their reference-matching coverage differ and change. Citation graph membership is evidence of a deposited/extracted bibliographic relation only. For mathematics and physics, arXiv is particularly important for preprints and version history, while DOI registries and publisher pages are needed to connect preprints to the final record. No API calls or paid operations were run for this research.
