# Summary

Canonical quantization is the operator-based route from a classical Hamiltonian field theory to a quantum field theory.

# Statement or Definition

One promotes the classical field variables and their conjugate momenta to operators and replaces classical Poisson brackets with operator commutators.

# Discussion

This approach mirrors ordinary quantum mechanics, but now the degrees of freedom are fields distributed over space rather than finitely many coordinates.

> [!quote] Tong, *Quantum Field Theory*, p. 21
> In field theory we do the same, now for the field $\phi^a(\vec{x})$ and its momentum conjugate $\pi^b(\vec{x})$.

Local file: [[02 Atlas/09 Quantum Field Theory/99 Sources/01 qft_david_tong.pdf|qft_david_tong.pdf]]

The main conceptual move is simple, but the resulting operator algebra sits inside a much larger framework involving mode expansions, vacua, particles, and interacting theories.

# Examples

- Quantization of the free scalar field.
- Promotion of equal-time Poisson brackets to equal-time commutators.
