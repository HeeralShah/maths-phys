# Summary

Quantum field theory is needed because relativistic physics does not preserve particle number and because identical quantum particles are most naturally understood as excitations of a common field.

# Statement or Definition

QFT replaces the fixed-particle-number framework of ordinary non-relativistic quantum mechanics with a field-based framework in which particles can be created, destroyed, and treated as genuinely identical excitations of the same underlying object.

# Discussion

Tong motivates QFT with two complementary ideas.

First, relativity and quantum mechanics together force us beyond a one-particle or fixed-particle description. Once energies are high enough, particle-antiparticle production becomes unavoidable, so a theory with a fixed number of particles is no longer enough.

Second, identical particles are not merely similar; they are genuinely indistinguishable. In ordinary quantum mechanics this leads us to impose symmetric or antisymmetric wavefunctions by hand. In QFT, that structure is built more naturally into the operator formalism for fields.

> [!quote] Tong, *Quantum Field Theory*, printed p. 3
> Swapping two particles around leaves the state completely unchanged - apart from a possible minus sign. This minus sign determines the statistics of the particle.

# Examples

- Pair production shows why particle number cannot remain fixed in a relativistic theory.
- Bose and Fermi statistics in ordinary quantum mechanics must be imposed manually by symmetrizing or antisymmetrizing multiparticle states.
